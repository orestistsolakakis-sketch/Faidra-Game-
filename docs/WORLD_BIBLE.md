# The Heart Engine — World Design Bible

> **Canonical worldbuilding document.** This is the single source of truth for
> the setting, its magic, its history, and its themes. All narrative, dialogue,
> quest, and level content must be consistent with this file. When lore evolves,
> it changes *here first*, then flows into the systems.

**Project type:** Narrative fantasy game — exploration, mystery, worldbuilding,
political history, character-driven storytelling.

**Genre:** Steampunk fantasy · Gothic fantasy · Narrative adventure · Mystery ·
Political fantasy · Romance · Exploration RPG.

---

## 1. The Core Idea of the World

The world is built on the relationship between three forces:

- **Life** — the natural world and living beings.
- **Magic** — the invisible energy flowing through the world.
- **Machinery** — the technology humans created to understand, control, and
  interact with magic.

The world became advanced because humans learned to **combine magic and
machinery**. But magic is not a simple energy source — it is connected to the
living world. The more humans try to control it, the more consequences they
create.

## 2. How Magic Works

Magic exists everywhere, but most people cannot consciously use it. It flows
through the world in invisible currents called **the Lifelines**, which run
through the earth, rivers, mountains, plants, animals, humans, and machinery.

The **Heart Engine** was created to regulate these currents.

Core rules of magic:

- Magic is **not infinite**. It must **move**.
- If magic becomes **trapped** in one location, it becomes **unstable**.
- If too much magic is **removed** from a living thing, that thing **weakens**.
- If too much magic is **forced into machinery**, the machinery begins behaving
  **unpredictably**.

## 3. The Forms of Magic

Magic is not divided into classical elements. It expresses itself through
**connections**. The most common forms:

| Form | Expression |
|---|---|
| **Life** | Healing, growth, regeneration, vitality |
| **Memory** | Dreams, forgotten experiences, inherited memories |
| **Emotion** | Fear, love, grief, anger, hope |
| **Fate** | Possibilities, visions, prophecy |
| **Identity** | Names, bloodlines, perception, belonging |
| **Connection** | Communication, bonds, shared experiences |
| **Sacrifice** | Giving something of yourself to preserve or change something |

These are not separate schools — they **overlap**. A healer does not simply
"restore health"; they may be reconnecting a person's life force to their body.

## 4. Healing Magic

Healing magic is **extremely rare**. Most believe it merely closes wounds. It
does not. A true healer senses the relationship between the **body**, the
**mind**, the person's **life force**, and the **magic** inside them. A healer
does not create life from nothing — they help the body restore itself.

**Limits.** A healer cannot bring back the dead, instantly regrow a missing
limb, remove every illness, or heal without spending their own energy.

**Cost.** Healing requires a transfer of energy. The more severe the injury, the
greater the cost — exhaustion, pain, temporary loss of ability, memory loss,
physical weakness. The most powerful healers can heal another's **life force**,
but doing so can **permanently damage their own**.

## 5. The Healer Bloodline

Centuries ago, a woman connected to the royal court possessed a rare form of
healing magic. She was not royal — she rose through her knowledge of medicine
and magic, and was eventually connected romantically to **King Ewald**. Their
children inherited her ability.

The ability appeared **differently in each child** — healing wounds, sensing
illness, healing magical damage, transferring pain, healing plants and animals.
The power was not always obvious; some descendants could live entire lives
without knowing they possessed it. It became visible only when the person
**learned to use it**.

## 6. Why the Bloodline Was Hidden

The royal family feared the healer bloodline — not because healing was evil, but
because it was **powerful**. The crown depended on healers during wars, plagues,
political conflicts, and magical disasters. A bloodline of healers tied to the
throne could become politically dangerous: people might come to see them as more
important than the monarchy.

So the family was **erased**. Official history claimed the woman and her children
disappeared. In truth, the descendants kept living throughout the kingdom, many
settling in lower-class districts as **doctors, midwives, herbalists, nurses,
battlefield medics, and mechanics who worked with biological machinery**. The
bloodline survived **without knowing what it was**.

## 7. History of the World

**The Age of Wild Magic.** Before civilization, magic was uncontrolled and the
world was dangerous — magic could reshape landscapes, spawn storms, alter
creatures, distort time, destroy settlements. Humans survived by learning where
magic was safe; early civilizations formed around **stable magical regions**.

**The Age of Discovery.** Humans studied magic and found that certain materials
conduct it — crystal, certain metals, ancient minerals, living plants, and human
blood. This produced the first magical machines.

**The Age of Machinery.** Technology developed rapidly: mechanical tools, magical
transportation, steam machinery, energy systems, medical devices, and
intelligence-like machines. But the more magic people used, the more **unstable**
the world became.

## 8. The First Crown

Eventually, **seven inventors and the first queen** realized the world could not
keep using magic without regulation. They built the **Heart Engine** to stabilize
the Lifelines. It did not create magic — it **regulated its movement**. It became
the foundation of modern civilization.

## 9. The Heart Engine

Hidden beneath the royal capital, the Heart Engine is a massive machine connected
to the world's Lifelines, controlling the flow of magical energy. It does not
merely need fuel — it needs **balance**. Its systems are connected to **Life,
Memory, Emotion, Fate, Identity, Connection, and Sacrifice**. The **Seven Royal
Relics** were created to interface with these systems.

## 10. The Seven Relics

The relics are **interfaces between humanity and the Heart Engine** — each
represents one part of what keeps the world stable.

| Relic | System |
|---|---|
| **Clockwork Heart Key** | Truth |
| **Whisper Coins** | Secrets and memory |
| **Hollow Lantern** | Emotion |
| **Steam Lily** | Life and intention |
| **Dream Thread** | Fate |
| **Queen's Pawn** | Identity and perception |
| **Bloodvine Bracelet** | Sacrifice and healing |

The relics need not be held by royalty — the royal family only *claimed* them
because they controlled the Heart Engine.

## 11. Why the Heart Engine Is Failing

The Engine was designed to be maintained by a **balance of different forms of
magic working together**. Over time the royal family became obsessed with
**control** — they stopped working *with* the world and tried to control it, and
the Engine became less balanced. They also **erased the healer bloodline**, one
of history's biggest mistakes: the original system required different magical
abilities working together, and separating the bloodlines left it **incomplete**.
The Engine kept functioning, but slowly **deteriorated**.

## 12. The World's Current Problems

The failure manifests differently by region — the problems *look* unrelated but
are all symptoms of the same cause.

- **The Crownspire** — magical machinery malfunctions; royal archives change;
  protective spells fail; people experience strange visions.
- **Gearmarket** — machines behave unpredictably; factory equipment turns
  dangerous; energy systems overload; inventors begin disappearing.
- **Cinder Hollow** — people fall sick; healing becomes harder; old underground
  machinery wakes; strange magical growth appears in the tunnels.

## 13. The Three Classes of Society

Society is divided by **access** — to education, medicine, technology, magic,
government, and information — not simply into "good poor" and "evil rich."

- **Upper class** — access to advanced magical technology.
- **Middle class** — creates and distributes much of the technology.
- **Lower class** — lives closest to the consequences of magical instability.

The central contradiction: the people with the **least political power** are
often the people **most affected** by the world's problems.

## 14. Technology

Powered by a combination of **steam, mechanical engineering, magical crystals,
conductive metals, and biological materials**. Technology should feel
**practical and imperfect** — machines break, need maintenance, make noise, leak
steam, overheat, need replacement parts. This is not a clean futuristic
steampunk world; it is **lived-in**: repaired repeatedly, buildings expanded over
older buildings, technology from different centuries coexisting.

## 15. Medicine

Two systems coexist:

- **Traditional medicine** — herbs, surgery, chemistry, mechanical tools,
  anatomy.
- **Magical healing** — uses a person's life force; rare and hereditary. Because
  it is hereditary, many healers don't know their gift is magical — they believe
  they are simply unusually talented doctors. This is how the hidden bloodline
  persists unseen throughout society.

## 16. The Biggest Lie in History

The royal family claims **the royal bloodline created the modern world**. The
truth: the modern world was created by **many people**, and the royal family
simply became the ones who **controlled the machine**. Official history erased
the original inventors, the healer bloodline, the workers who maintained the
Engine, and the people who built the first cities. History as recorded is
therefore **incomplete** — the story is not about discovering an ancient machine,
but about discovering the entire world was built on an **incomplete version of
history**.

## 17. The Main Story of the World

The world is beginning to fail. At first nobody knows why; different communities
experience different problems. Governments blame terrorists, inventors, magic
users, poor districts, foreign nations, ancient curses. The truth is older: the
**Heart Engine is failing**, and those who understand it are dead, silenced,
forgotten, or separated from one another. The solution is **not** simply to
restart the Engine — the world must understand **why it became unstable in the
first place**.

## 18. Central Themes

- **Who gets to write history?** The official version is not always the true one.
- **Can something built to protect become a tool of control?** The Engine was
  made to save the world; the monarchy used its existence to justify its power.
- **Is bloodline destiny?** The healer bloodline inherits ability — but ability
  does not determine who someone becomes.
- **What does healing actually mean?** Not always restoring what was; sometimes
  healing means adapting, accepting change, rebuilding, reconnecting.

## Canon Note — The Two Bloodlines

The affair-woman's descendants are **not** secretly "more royal" than the
princess's family. Instead:

- The **official royal bloodline** inherited the **authority and responsibility**
  of the Heart Engine.
- The **healer bloodline** inherited a rare **biological/magical ability**.
- Both branches are important to the world's history.
- **Neither branch is complete by itself.**

The story is therefore about **two parts of history that were separated**, rather
than one "true" family and one "fake" one.
